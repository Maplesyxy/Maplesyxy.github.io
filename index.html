01About

I’m a first-year at Yale studying Math & Computer Science and Economics, a Hillhouse Fellow, and a tech sector analyst in the Yale Student Investment Group.

What I most want to work on is learning in games: architectures and training frameworks where an agent’s objective is set by other adaptive agents rather than by a fixed dataset. Static benchmarks like ARC-AGI measure a model once, against a frozen target. Multi-agent settings move the target: the best response changes as opponents learn, equilibria shift, and generalization means adapting online to a strategy you have never seen. I’m interested in model classes built for that regime, combining game-theoretic solution concepts with deep learning, learned opponent models and self-play curricula, so that evaluation itself becomes adaptive instead of a one-shot score.

My background is computational biology and medical ML: a GVP-GCN + Transformer for RNA inverse folding, an interpretable Grad-CAM Vision Transformer for early Alzheimer’s diagnosis, a sparse event-camera ViT, and a summer in a liver-cancer lab designing antisense oligonucleotides. Before Yale I graduated cum laude from The Lawrenceville School and was named a 2026 U.S. Presidential Scholar; the things I founded there live under Organizations.

03Research & Publications

  1. 2025

    Deep Learning Framework for RNA Inverse Folding with Geometric Structure Potentials

    A. Yao · arXiv:2601.00895 · Highlights in Science, Engineering and Technology (BLSME 2026)

    A GVP-GCN + Transformer that designs RNA sequences folding into a target 3D backbone. Curated 6,300+ PDB structures (deduplicated to ~2,500 with CD-HIT) and reached 0.481 recovery / 0.332 TM-score on RNA-Puzzles, ahead of RDesign, RiboLogic and LEARNA. ISEF 2025 research.

    arXiv Poster Code

  2. 2025

    Targeting NELFE Splicing with Antisense Oligonucleotides in Hepatocellular Carcinoma

    A. Yao, L. Reynolds, H. Dang · Dang Lab, Thomas Jefferson University · report and poster

    A step toward a new treatment for liver cancer. Hepatocellular carcinoma is the most common liver cancer and one of the deadliest, and the RNA-binding protein NELFE drives its growth. We designed antisense oligonucleotides that force NELFE exon-10 skipping, mimicking a naturally protective variant, and showed they lower NELFE protein in Huh1 and Huh7 cells: the crucial first step before delivering the ASOs to tumors in vivo. Data from 44 PCRs, 10 Western blots and five weeks of IVIS imaging; first-author 13-page report.

    Poster Dang Lab

  3. 2025

    Investigation into US Citizen and Non-Citizen Worker Health Insurance and Employment

    A. Yao · arXiv:2601.00896

    An empirical study of health-insurance coverage and employment outcomes across citizen and non-citizen workers in the United States, using χ² and two-proportion tests, K-Prototypes clustering, t-SNE and a CatBoost classifier.

    arXiv

  4. 2024

    Enhancing Real-Time Vision Systems: Integrating Dynamic Vision Sensors with Vision Transformers to Increase Computational Efficiency

    A. Yao, O. Su · 2nd International Conference on Machine Learning and Automation · Applied and Computational Engineering

    A custom ViT for sparse DVS128 Gesture event data that exploits temporal sparsity, reaching 98.4% accuracy in five epochs against spiking-network baselines averaging 93.1%.

  5. 2024
