01About

I’m a first-year at Yale studying Math & Computer Science and Economics, a Hillhouse Fellow, and an analyst in the technology sector of the Yale Student Investment Group. I like creating new things and am almost always working on a project.

My research so far has lived at the intersection of deep learning and the sciences: a GVP-GCN + Transformer for RNA inverse folding that beat RDesign, RiboLogic and LEARNA on RNA-Puzzles, a custom Vision Transformer for sparse event-camera data, and an interpretable Grad-CAM ViT for early Alzheimer’s diagnosis from MRI. As a quant research intern I applied the same toolkit to financial signal prediction.

Lately I’ve been drawn to learning in games: how deep learning and game theory meet when agents have to plan against each other. With teammates I’ve been building competitive bots for Citadel’s Terminal and Cambridge Battlecode, including fast combat simulators, opponent build-order predictors, and self-play win matrices of hundreds of games that decide which strategy ships. I’m taking Game Theory and ML in Algorithmic Trading alongside, and I want to push this toward multi-agent reinforcement learning.

Before Yale I graduated cum laude from The Lawrenceville School as a Hutchins Jefferson Science Scholar and was named a 2026 U.S. Presidential Scholar. I also founded a student science publication and a STEM nonprofit; those live under Organizations.

    Deep Learning Framework for RNA Inverse Folding with Geometric Structure Potentials

    A. Yao · arXiv:2601.00895 · Highlights in Science, Engineering and Technology (BLSME 2026)

    A. Yao · arXiv:2601.00896

  3. 2025

    Splice-switching antisense oligonucleotides against NELFE in hepatocellular carcinoma

    A. Yao, with the Dang Lab · Thomas Jefferson University · internal report and poster

    Wet-lab project testing whether ASOs that force NELFE exon-10 skipping reproduce the effect of a protective SNP in liver cancer cells and mouse models. Data from 44 PCRs, 10 Western blots and five weeks of IVIS imaging; first-author 13-page report presented at the Lawrenceville science showcase.
